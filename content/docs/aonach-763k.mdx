This is the whole Rós Madair bet, at scale: **763,064 buildings, 30,000 people, 42 wards —
~6.98 million tiles, 1.3 GB of Parquet — emitted through the real engine and served as static
files.** No database, no backend. The page below queries it in your browser.

The corpus is believable, not filler: every building sits on a **real OSM footprint**, its
typology sampled from that footprint's land-use zone; names are addresses or Irish place-names, not
`Building 0001`; and the critical services stay a realistic handful — **8 hospitals, 2 emergency
centres**, city-wide — not scaled with the replica count.

How the question stays cheap over 7M tiles — every step prunes on **sort order**, the only thing
Parquet gives without bloom filters:

- **Spatial prunes in the substrate.** Each building is bucketed into a whole-world **Z-order grid
  cell**, written once to a cell-sorted edge (`grid_<graph>.parquet`). The box → its overlapping
  cells is pure arithmetic (`cellsInBox`, the *same* wasm contract emit wrote the cells with), and
  `cell IN (…)` reads only those cells' row groups. No R-tree download, no tile scan.
- **The concept filter prunes.** `Concept(service_roles Is "Hospital")` is the *same* IR→SQL the
  native engine runs, lowered by the `ros-madair` wasm compiler and executed by duckdb-wasm over
  the melted concept store — a node-scoped, exact semijoin that touches the role's rows, not the
  2.4M-row store, and certainly not the 6.98M tiles. `All[GridBox, Concept]` is one compiled
  `INTERSECT`: both sides prune.
- **Rendering rides the same scan.** Each result's name and ward come back **in the cell-pruned
  grid read itself** (`render_summary`, the compact related-title blob emit carried on the grid
  edge). The render lands on the exact rows the spatial query already touched — no scattered second
  fetch, no fat tile partition.

Spatial prunes, the graph query prunes, the render rides along: the answer touches a sliver of the
1.3 GB, which is why it returns in the browser at all.
